What This Demo Shows

This demo answers a single question: How do you reconstruct decision state historically when models evolve?

Every AI-driven cash application decision — matching payments to invoices, approving tolerance matches, applying credit memos — directly affects revenue recognition and is subject to SOX audit. When an auditor or Controller needs to understand why a period closed the way it did, they need two distinct answers:

System Time (Transaction Time)

What did the system know at the moment it made the decision? Reconstructs the exact database state — invoices, proposals, model versions, actors — as it existed at a specific past timestamp.

Valid Time (Business Time)

What was true in the business world at that time, even if the system didn't know it yet? Credit memos, remittance corrections, and pricing adjustments often arrive late.

Why XTDB? No conventional database handles both of these as first-class queryable capabilities. XTDB handles both natively — no custom event sourcing, no log reconstruction, no application-level temporal modeling.

Scenario 1 — Reconstruct Decision State

On February 13 at 4:30 PM EST, a $10,000 payment arrives from Acme Corp with a dirty remittance: PAYMENT REF ACME FEB INVOICES CONSOLIDATED PMT. No invoice numbers. The AI matching engine (model v1.2) proposes a composite match across three open invoices summing to $10,000. An AR Analyst approves. The batch goes to NetSuite.

In April, an auditor asks: What did you know on February 13? What did you decide? Why? What changed afterward?

Key Point Every query below uses FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF anchored to the same timestamp. The result is a complete reconstruction of the decision state as the system understood it at that exact moment.

1A Decision state at Feb 13 4:30 PM

Joins invoices, the decision journal, and the match proposal — all anchored to a single transaction-time snapshot.

SELECT
  inv._id             AS invoice_id,
  inv.status,
  inv.amount,
  dj._id              AS decision_id,
  dj.outcome,
  dj.actor_role,
  dj.reason_summary,
  mp.proposal_type,
  mp.confidence,
  mp.model_version
FROM invoice FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF TIMESTAMP '2026-02-13T21:30:00Z' inv
JOIN decision_journal FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF TIMESTAMP '2026-02-13T21:30:00Z' dj
  ON dj.payment_id = 'P-9001'
JOIN match_proposal FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF TIMESTAMP '2026-02-13T21:30:00Z' mp
  ON mp._id = dj.proposal_id
WHERE inv.merchant_id = 'M-100'
  AND dj.outcome = 'APPROVED'

1B Same snapshot with payment details

Adds the payment's payer name, raw remittance text, and channel to show the auditor the full picture.

SELECT
  inv._id             AS invoice_id,
  inv.status,
  inv.amount,
  p.payer_name,
  p.raw_ref_text,
  p.channel,
  dj._id              AS decision_id,
  dj.outcome,
  dj.actor_role,
  mp.proposal_type,
  mp.confidence,
  mp.model_version
FROM decision_journal FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF TIMESTAMP '2026-02-13T21:30:00Z' dj
JOIN payment FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF TIMESTAMP '2026-02-13T21:30:00Z' p
  ON p._id = dj.payment_id
JOIN match_proposal FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF TIMESTAMP '2026-02-13T21:30:00Z' mp
  ON mp._id = dj.proposal_id
JOIN invoice FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF TIMESTAMP '2026-02-13T21:30:00Z' inv
  ON inv.merchant_id = 'M-100'
WHERE dj.payment_id = 'P-9001'
  AND dj.outcome = 'APPROVED'

1C Audit chain — did the decision reach NetSuite?

Closes the audit loop: the approved decision was submitted as a manager entry upload batch to NetSuite.

SELECT
  dj._id              AS decision_id,
  dj.outcome,
  dj.actor_role,
  eb._id              AS batch_id,
  eb.erp,
  eb.batch_type,
  eb.submitted_by,
  eb.status
FROM decision_journal FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF TIMESTAMP '2026-02-13T21:30:00Z' dj
JOIN erp_upload_batch FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF TIMESTAMP '2026-02-13T21:30:00Z' eb
  ON eb.submitted_by = dj.actor_user_id
WHERE dj.payment_id = 'P-9001'
  AND dj.outcome = 'APPROVED'

2 Model version comparison

No temporal qualifier — both model versions exist in the current decision journal.

SELECT
  mp.model_version,
  mp.confidence,
  mp.proposal_type,
  dj.outcome,
  dj.reason_summary
FROM match_proposal mp
JOIN decision_journal dj ON dj.proposal_id = mp._id
WHERE mp.payment_ids = 'P-9001'
ORDER BY mp.model_version

Scenario 2 — Backdated Credit Memo

A $200 credit memo on invoice I-7003. The sales team agreed to the pricing adjustment verbally on February 24. The documentation arrived and was entered into the system on March 3.

An auditor asks: Why did you close that invoice without accounting for the credit memo?

The correct answer requires two separate queries — one for what the system knew, and one for what was actually true.

The Structural Gap This scenario happens at every enterprise AR shop at month-end. Facts arrive late. The system made a defensible decision based on what it knew — but the business reality was different. This gap is completely unsolvable without bitemporal data modeling.

3A System Time — Feb 25

What did the system have recorded by February 25? The credit memo was entered on March 3 — from the system's perspective, it does not exist yet.

SELECT
  _id          AS cm_id,
  invoice_id,
  amount,
  reason,
  issued_date
FROM credit_memo
  FOR SYSTEM_TIME AS OF
    TIMESTAMP '2026-02-25T16:00:00Z'
WHERE invoice_id = 'I-7003'
Loading
Zero rows. Operationally defensible — the system didn't know about the credit memo when the decision was made.

3B Valid Time — Feb 25

What was true in the business world on February 25? The credit memo was effective February 24 — it existed as a business fact.

SELECT
  _id          AS cm_id,
  invoice_id,
  amount,
  reason,
  issued_date
FROM credit_memo
  FOR VALID_TIME AS OF
    TIMESTAMP '2026-02-25T16:00:00Z'
WHERE invoice_id = 'I-7003'

One row. The credit memo existed in the business world. XTDB surfaces it via valid-time query while system-time correctly shows the system didn't know.
